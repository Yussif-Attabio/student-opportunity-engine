Student Opportunity Engine — Live Demo Script

Opening (30s)

“Hi — thanks for joining. Students today juggle dozens of places to find internships, scholarships, research roles, campus jobs and events. It’s easy to miss a great opportunity or lose track of applications.

Quick solution overview (30s)

“This demo shows the Student Opportunity Engine, a lightweight prototype that brings opportunities into one personalized feed. You edit a simple profile, the app scores and explains matches, and you can search, save, track status, and see upcoming deadlines — all in the browser.

Demo flow (4–6 minutes)

1) Start on the Opportunities / Dashboard page
- "I’m on the Opportunities tab. Up top is a small dashboard: total opportunities, how many I’ve saved, how many are due soon (within 14 days), and the highest match score in the current dataset. That helper tip points out you can edit your profile to improve matches."

2) Show the Profile tab and explain personalization (30–45s)
- "Now I’ll go to the Profile tab. This is the heart of personalization: name, school, major, year, comma-separated interests and skills, preferred opportunity types, location, availability and goals."
- "As I edit interests or skills, the matching updates immediately — meaning the list is personalized to what you enter here."

3) Return to Opportunities and inspect matched cards (45–60s)
- "Back in Opportunities, cards are sorted by match score. Each card shows title, source, location, deadline, a short description, required skills, and a small match badge with the percentage."
- "There’s also a short list of match reasons (two or three bullets) that explain why the app thinks this is a fit — handy when evaluating quickly."

4) Use search and filters (30–45s)
- "Here’s the search box — it scans title, source, description, skills, tags, type, and location. You can also filter by opportunity type, location, and deadline urgency (due soon / due later)."
- "I’ll type a keyword or pick a filter to narrow results so you can see how that behaves." (Perform a sample filter)

5) Open the Learn More modal (30–45s)
- "Click ‘Learn More’ to open the details modal. This shows full description, required skills, related majors, tags, and the next application step."

6) Show AI Application Guidance (30–45s)
- "Inside the modal there’s an “AI Application Guidance” section. Important note: this is local demo guidance — no real AI calls. It gives four short items: why this opp is worth applying to, a suggested next step, what to highlight in your application, and one resume/cover letter tip. It pulls from your profile, matched skills/tags, and the opportunity details to make practical suggestions."

7) Save an opportunity (15–20s)
- "Tap the Save button on a card or in the modal to bookmark it. Saved items persist to your browser via localStorage, so they stay across reloads in this demo."

8) Go to Saved tab and update application status (30–45s)
- "Now open Saved. Each saved opportunity shows the same card layout and a status dropdown. Supported statuses: Saved, Applying, Applied, Interview, Done. Change a status and it persists. The status badge color gives quick visual feedback."

9) Go to Deadlines tab and show deadline tracking (30–45s)
- "In Deadlines we show upcoming deadlines sorted soonest-first, with urgency badges: Due soon (≤14 days), Upcoming (>14 days), or Past. There’s an option to show only saved deadlines so you can focus on what you’re tracking."

Closing (30s)

"That’s the core of the Student Opportunity Engine demo. It’s a simple, local prototype showing how a profile can drive personalized matching, help you evaluate opportunities quickly, and track application progress and deadlines. Next steps would be real opportunity integrations, richer AI-driven recommendations, calendar reminders, and school/partner dashboards. Happy to demo any part in more depth or walk through the code." 

Presentation tips
- Keep profile edits short and focused on 2–3 key skills/interests to see clear match changes.
- Use the dashboard to highlight impact: changing profile should increase Highest match and change the feed order.
- Mention that saved items and statuses are stored locally — no account required for the demo.

End of script — have fun presenting!
